This page exists because "which chaps should I buy" is the most common
question a new volunteer asks, and the honest answer is a standard, not a
product.

<Note label="How this list is ordered">
By what the gear has to do, then by what it has to meet. Where products are
named at all, they are named as examples of a specification — not because they
pay better. Nothing on this page appears in a different order than it would
if there were no commissions at all, and there are no commercial links on any
life-safety module.
</Note>

<Block kicker="The list, in priority order">

If you buy in this order, you always own the most useful next thing.

1. **Chaps or chainsaw trousers** meeting **ASTM F1897**, or UL certified.
   Front and side coverage, long enough to reach the boot. This is the single
   highest-value purchase in chainsaw work and the one people skip.
2. **A trauma kit** — tourniquet ×2, hemostatic or packing gauze, pressure
   bandage, gloves, shears. Then **take a Stop the Bleed class**, because the
   kit is worthless as an object.
3. **Helmet with mesh face screen and ear defenders.** The combined
   forestry-helmet systems are cheap and work.
4. **Safety glasses**, worn under the screen.
5. **Boots** with a toe cap, a high ankle and aggressive tread. Chainsaw-rated
   boots exist and are better; a good work boot is the floor.
6. **Gloves** that fit. Grip first.
7. **Two to four spare chains per saw**, semi-chisel, correct pitch and gauge.
   More useful per dollar than any other saw accessory.
8. **Files, a file guide and a raker gauge** for the chain you own.
9. **Plastic felling wedges ×2** and a hammer or axe to drive them.
10. **Fuel and bar oil cans**, marked, plus a measured mixing bottle — or buy
    canned pre-mix and skip the mixing.
11. **A scrench, spare plug, spare E-clip, washer, bar nuts and chain catcher**
    in a small bag that lives with the saw.

</Block>

<Block kicker="Where to buy on Kaua‘i">

Buy locally where you can. A dealer who knows you will get to your saw faster
after the next storm, and that is worth more than a discount.

- **Your nearest saw dealer** for anything STIHL, for chains cut to length, for
  bar work, and for advice. Ask which shops the working crews use.
- **Local hardware and farm supply** for fuel cans, bar oil, wedges, files and
  gloves.
- **Online** for PPE sizing ranges, trauma kits and consumables the island
  runs out of after a storm.

<Hazard label="Two things not to buy online">
**Gray-market STIHL saws.** Third-party sellers, no dealer warranty, no
support. If you want a STIHL, buy it from a dealer.

**Unrated chaps.** "Chainsaw style" chaps with no ASTM or UL marking on the
label do nothing. The standard is the product.
</Hazard>

</Block>

<Block kicker="Used gear">

Worth saying plainly: **the used professional saw in your uncle's garage is
probably a better saw than a new homeowner model at the same price.** Better
case, better AV mounts, rebuildable, designed to run for hours.

What to check on a used saw: compression on the pull, clean air filter seal,
bar rails not spread, sprocket wear, a chain brake that actually stops the
chain, no cracks around the handle mounts and the tank seam, and whether the
oiler throws a line.

What not to buy used: **chaps and helmets.** Protective gear has a service
life and you cannot see the damage.

</Block>
