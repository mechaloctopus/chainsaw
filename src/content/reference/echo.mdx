ECHO is heavily represented on this island, and for good reasons that have
nothing to do with brand loyalty: the dealer support is real, the value is
good, and unlike STIHL you can actually buy one online or off a shelf when
yours dies in the middle of a cleanup.

<Note label="Verify the current lineup">
Model lineups change every year or two, displacements get revised, and models
get discontinued. Everything below is a guide to reading the range, not a
current catalogue. Check ECHO's own site or your dealer before you buy, and
read [the unverified list](/about/verify/) for why this site flags rather than
guesses.
</Note>

<Block kicker="How to read the numbers">

`CS` is a chainsaw. The number that follows tracks engine displacement in
cubic centimeters, more or less: a CS-490 is around 50cc, a CS-590 around
60cc. `T` on the end means top handle. `P` usually marks a
higher-specification version in the same displacement class.

So the number tells you roughly how much saw it is, and unlike the STIHL
letter system it has stayed fairly consistent.

</Block>

<Block kicker="What turns up on crews here">

| Model | Class | Honest assessment for storm work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CS-310, CS-352 | Light, ~30–34cc | Limbing and small stuff. Cheap, easy to handle, out of their depth in a big albizia. Fine as a second saw. |
| CS-490 | ~50cc | **The sweet spot for most volunteers.** Enough saw for most of what is on the ground, light enough to carry all day in the heat. |
| CS-590 Timber Wolf | ~60cc | **The workhorse.** The saw you will see most on cleanup crews on this island. Heavy enough to notice, and it will pull a 20-inch bar through anything a volunteer should be cutting. |
| CS-620P | ~60cc+ | More power in the same class. Good if you have the wrists for it. |
| CS-2511T | ~25cc top handle | **Roped, trained arborists only.** A top-handle saw is designed for one-handed use in a tree. In untrained hands it is the most dangerous saw sold. |
| Battery (DCS series) | — | Quiet, no mix, instant start, genuinely useful for small limbing near houses. Understand that chaps behave differently with a high-torque electric drive — see [module 01](/modules/01-ppe-and-the-buddy-rule/). |

</Block>

<Block kicker="Why the buying story is different from STIHL">

STIHL sells only through its authorized dealer network, and dealers are not
permitted to ship new saws to you. That has a practical consequence: if your
saw dies mid-cleanup, you go to a dealer, in person, during opening hours,
and hope they have stock.

ECHO is sold through dealers **and** through big-box and online retail. After
a storm, when every dealer on the island is three weeks deep in repairs, that
difference is the difference between working and waiting.

It also means the commercial incentives around the two brands are different,
and this site would rather say that out loud than pretend it is not a factor.
See the [editorial policy](/about/editorial-policy/).

</Block>

<Block kicker="Warranty">

ECHO's consumer warranty terms have historically been long for the category,
with a shorter period for commercial or rental use. The terms depend on the
model, the year and how you use it, and registering the saw usually matters.

Read the card that came in the box. If you are cutting for anyone other than
yourself — even unpaid, even for a church group — check whether that counts as
commercial use for your warranty, because it sometimes does.

</Block>

<Block kicker="Buy local anyway, when you can">

A dealer who knows you is worth more than a few dollars saved online. They
sharpen chains, they diagnose a saw in five minutes, they have the E-clip you
lost, and after the next storm they will get to your saw sooner because they
know your face.

Kaua‘i needs functioning dealers more than this site needs a commission.
</Block>
