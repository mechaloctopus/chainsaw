Terminology on this site is fixed. A sawyer is always a sawyer, never an
"operator" on one page and a "cutter" on the next — because the day you are
reading this at speed, in the field, is the day inconsistency costs you.

<Block kicker="The crew">

**Sawyer.** The person running the saw. Not "operator", not "cutter".

**Swamper.** The second person. Moves cut material clear, opens the work face,
watches the sawyer's back, keeps the escape path open, calls the stop. Not
"helper", not "assistant" — the job has a name and it is a real job.

**The nod.** The rule that the swamper does not enter the sawyer's work zone
until the sawyer makes eye contact and nods him in, with the saw braked or
off first. See [module 10](/modules/10-two-person-saw-team/).

**Tank for tank.** Swapping sawyer and swamper roles each time the saw runs
out of fuel — roughly every 20 to 30 minutes.

**Work zone.** The area inside which the bar could reach, plus the area
material is moving into. Roughly a bar length plus a body length, larger
whenever anything is loaded.

**Drop zone.** Where a tree or piece is going to land, plus everything it
could reach. Bystanders stay two tree lengths back.

**Stop work.** A call anyone on site can make, for any reason, that stops the
saw immediately. It is never questioned in the moment.

</Block>

<Block kicker="The saw">

**Powerhead.** The engine and handles — the saw without the bar and chain.

**Guide bar / bar.** The steel plate the chain runs around. Stamped with pitch,
gauge and drive link count.

**Chain.** Cutters, tie straps and drive links riveted into a loop.

**Cutter.** One tooth. Has a top plate, a side plate, and a working corner
where the two meet.

**Depth gauge / raker.** The ramp in front of each cutter that limits how big
a bite it takes. Two names, same part; this site says "raker" in the field and
"depth gauge" when talking about the setting.

**Pitch.** Distance between three consecutive rivets, divided by two.
<span class="spec">1/4</span>, <span class="spec">.325</span>,
<span class="spec">3/8 LP</span>, <span class="spec">3/8</span>,
<span class="spec">.404</span> inches.

**Gauge.** Thickness of the drive link that rides in the bar groove.
<span class="spec">.043</span>, <span class="spec">.050</span>,
<span class="spec">.058</span>, <span class="spec">.063</span> inches.

**Drive link (DL).** The tang under the chain that rides in the groove and
takes the drive from the sprocket. Counted per loop.

**Sprocket.** Drives the chain. **Rim** type is a replaceable ring on splines;
**spur** type is machined into the clutch drum.

**Clutch cover.** The plate over the sprocket, clutch and brake band, which
also clamps the bar.

**Chain brake.** The band that clamps the clutch drum to stop the chain, fired
by the handguard or by inertia.

**Chain catcher.** The finger under the bar that catches a thrown chain before
it reaches your hand.

**Bumper spikes / dogs.** The toothed edge at the bar root that bites the wood
so you pivot the saw through the cut.

**Scrench.** Combination bar wrench and screwdriver.

**AV mounts.** Anti-vibration mounts isolating the powerhead from the handles.

**Semi-chisel / full chisel.** Rounded versus square working corner.
Semi-chisel holds an edge in dirty wood and is the storm-cleanup chain.

**Kerf.** The slot the chain cuts.

</Block>

<Block kicker="Cutting">

**Bucking.** Cutting a felled or downed trunk into lengths.

**Limbing.** Removing branches from a downed trunk.

**Bind.** The load stored in a piece of wood by the way it is supported.

**Compression side.** Where the wood is being squeezed. Cut first; it closes on
the bar.

**Tension side.** Where the wood is being pulled apart. Cut second; it splits
open and the piece drops.

**Bore cut.** Cutting with the nose of the bar into the middle of the wood.
A nose technique — high skill, high consequence.

**Face cut / front cut.** The notch removed from the side the tree will fall
toward. **Open face** around 70°, **conventional** around 45°, **Humboldt** cut
up into the stump.

**Hinge / holding wood.** The strip of uncut wood left between face and back
cut. The only thing steering the tree. Roughly 10% of diameter, even
thickness, never cut through.

**Back cut.** The cut that releases the tree, leaving the hinge.

**Dutchman.** A face cut whose two cuts do not meet, leaving a step. Throws
the tree off line. A failure, not a blemish.

**Barber chair.** The trunk splitting vertically and the butt rearing up and
back off the stump. Lethal. Common in brittle wood — albizia.

**DBH.** Diameter at breast height. How tree diameter is normally quoted.

**Escape route.** Cleared ground, roughly 45° back from the felling direction,
15–20 feet, cleared before any cut.

</Block>

<Block kicker="Storm hazards">

**Spring pole.** A sapling or limb bent under load, storing energy. Shave the
outside of the bend at the apex; stand outside the arc.

**Root ball / rootwad.** The torn-up root plate of a partly uprooted tree, held
over by the weight of the trunk. Sever the trunk and it slams back into the
hole.

**Widow maker / hanger.** A broken limb or top caught in the canopy. Comes down
on its own schedule. A stop-work item.

**Hung-up tree.** A tree that fell partway and is leaning on another tree.
A refuse-the-job item for volunteers.

**Bridging.** A log that looks like it is lying on the ground and is actually
supported at both ends.

</Block>

<Block kicker="Electrical">

**Conductor.** A power line. This site calls every wire a conductor and treats
every conductor as energized.

**Service drop.** The line from the pole to a building. A conductor like any
other.

**Recloser.** An automatic device that re-energizes a circuit after a fault, on
its own, without warning.

**Backfeed.** Power pushed from a generator back out through a building's
service into the line outside, making a "dead" line live.

**Step potential / ground gradient.** Voltage spread through the ground around
a downed conductor. Why you shuffle out with your feet together instead of
walking.

</Block>

<Block kicker="Words this site will not use">

Because they suggest something that did not happen:

- **Certified**, **qualified**, **trained**, **approved**, **licensed** — about
  you, on the basis of this site. Nothing here confers any of those.
- **Certificate**, **badge**, **pass**, **score**, **level**.

What it says instead: **oriented**, **familiar with**, **has reviewed**.

</Block>
