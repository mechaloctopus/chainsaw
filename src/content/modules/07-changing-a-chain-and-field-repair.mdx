<Block kicker="The swap">

<Checklist id="chain-swap">
  <Check n="1" why="Engine off. Brake released — you cannot get the cover off with the brake set on most saws, and forcing it breaks the linkage.">
    Saw off, chain brake **released**.
  </Check>
  <Check n="2" why="Put them in your pocket, not on the ground and not on the bumper. Red dirt swallows a bar nut in one second.">
    Bar nuts off, cover off.
  </Check>
  <Check n="3" why="Back the tensioner off so the bar can slide back toward the sprocket.">
    Slack the tensioner.
  </Check>
  <Check n="4" why="Chain off the bar nose first, then off the sprocket.">
    Chain off.
  </Check>
  <Check n="5" why="This is the two minutes that makes the swap worth doing: groove scraped out, oil holes poked clear, drive pad wiped, brake band area cleaned of packed sawdust.">
    **Clean the bar groove and the oil holes.**
  </Check>
  <Check n="6" why="Wear marks under the tangs, and the E-clip and washer still on the drum. A missing E-clip is why saws come home in two pieces.">
    Check the sprocket, E-clip and washer.
  </Check>
  <Check n="7" why="Flip it if you are fitting a new chain. Bars wear on one rail.">
    Bar back on, or flipped.
  </Check>
  <Check n="8" why="The cutters on the TOP of the bar point forward, toward the nose. Get this backwards and the saw will run, vibrate, make dust, and cut nothing — people do it, and then blame the chain.">
    Chain on, **cutters pointing forward along the top**.
  </Check>
  <Check n="9" why="Drive links into the groove all the way around, including around the nose, and the tensioner pin into its hole in the bar.">
    Seat the chain and the tensioner pin.
  </Check>
  <Check n="10" why="Nose held up, tensioner snug, drive links staying in the groove when you pull a cutter away from the bar.">
    Tension with the nose held up.
  </Check>
  <Check n="11" why="Firm. Then start the saw, run it for thirty seconds, stop, and re-tension. A new chain stretches immediately.">
    Bar nuts tight, then **re-tension warm**.
  </Check>
  <Check n="12" why="Oil line off the bar nose. If it isn't throwing after a chain change, you blocked the oil hole putting it back together.">
    Confirm the oiler is throwing.
  </Check>
</Checklist>

<Block kicker="Carry spares. This is the single best piece of gear advice on this site.">

**Two to four sharp chains per saw**, in a tub or a zip bag in the truck.

Storm wood eats chains in ways clean timber does not. A rock in a root ball,
a nail in a plantation-era fence tree, a length of old cable grown into the
trunk, a chunk of concrete in a hedge row — and the chain is done instantly.

Swapping a chain: **three minutes**. Filing that chain in the field, well
enough to cut straight: **twenty minutes**, and the result will not be as
good as the one you sharpened at the bench.

Sharpen at home. Swap in the field. The crew keeps working.

</Block>

<Block kicker="Why chains come off">

A thrown chain is never random. It has one of these causes, and all of them
are visible beforehand.

<FailureModes kicker="Every cause, and its tell">
  <Fail tell="Chain sags below the bar when you lift the nose. The most common cause by a mile, and it develops as the chain warms.">
    Loose tension.
  </Fail>
  <Fail tell="Wear troughs cut into the sprocket teeth where the drive link tangs ride. A new chain on a worn sprocket will throw within the hour.">
    Worn sprocket.
  </Fail>
  <Fail tell="Squeeze the rails at mid-bar: if a drive link rocks side to side, the rails are spread and nothing will hold the chain straight.">
    Worn or spread bar rails.
  </Fail>
  <Fail tell="Shiny rub marks on the side of the bar and a chain that only throws when you are in a deep cut. You are levering the bar sideways.">
    Twisting the bar in the cut.
  </Fail>
  <Fail tell="A bent or bright-polished drive link tang. One damaged link will climb out of the groove on the nose every time it comes round.">
    A damaged drive link.
  </Fail>
  <Fail tell="A bright nick or a flat spot on the cutters and a sound you will remember. After metal contact, take the chain off and look at it properly.">
    Hitting wire, nails or concrete.
  </Fail>
</FailureModes>

</Block>

<Block kicker="Twisting the bar">

Worth its own section because it is the mistake that wrecks the most gear and
the one people do not know they are making.

**What it is:** levering the saw sideways in the cut — prying a limb up with
the bar, cutting with the side of the bar, forcing a cut that is closing, or
twisting the powerhead to correct a curving cut.

**What it does:** spreads the bar rails, bends the drive links, throws the
chain, and in a bad case puts a permanent bend in the bar.

**What to do instead:**

- Let the saw cut at its own speed. If it needs force, it is dull or the
  oiler is out.
- Plan the cut so it opens rather than closes. Read the bind
  ([module 13](/modules/13-bucking-and-limbing/)).
- Use **wedges** to hold a cut open. Plastic, always.
- If the bar is already pinched, stop and get it out properly — do not lever.

<Hazard label="A pinched saw">
Stop the engine first. Then: drive a plastic wedge into the cut above the
bar, lift the log with a lever that is not your saw, or make a second cut
from another angle with another saw. Never pry with the powerhead, and never
run the saw to try to force it free.
</Hazard>

</Block>

<Block kicker="The field kit">

Everything here fits in one small tool bag, and every item is something a
crew has needed in the middle of a job.

<Checklist id="field-kit" ordered={false}>
  <Check id="scrench" why="The bar wrench and screwdriver in one. Bring two — they get lost.">Scrench ×2</Check>
  <Check id="chains" why="Sharp, labelled with the saw they fit.">Spare chains ×2–4 per saw</Check>
  <Check id="files" why="Round file, flat file, raker gauge, handle. For touch-ups, not full sharpenings.">Files and a depth gauge tool</Check>
  <Check id="plug" why="Three dollars, ends a day when you don't have it.">Spare spark plug</Check>
  <Check id="clips" why="The parts that fall into the grass and end the job.">Spare E-clip, washer, bar nuts, chain catcher</Check>
  <Check id="wedges" why="Plastic. Two, not one. Never steel anywhere near a chain.">Plastic wedges ×2 and a hammer or axe</Check>

  <Check id="rags" why="Rags, a brush for the filter, zip ties, tape, gloves.">Rags, brush, zip ties</Check>

</Checklist>
