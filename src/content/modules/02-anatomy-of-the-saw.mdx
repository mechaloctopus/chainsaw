Learning part names is not trivia. When your partner shouts "chain catcher's
gone," you need to already know what that means for the next ten minutes.

Tap any part for its job and the way it fails.

<SawAnatomy />

<Block kicker="The four that decide whether you get hurt">

Of everything above, four parts are the difference between a bad day and a
very bad day, and all four are things you check by hand in under a minute.

1. **The chain brake.** Test it. Not look at it — run the saw, engage the
   brake, confirm the chain stops. Every day. A brake band packed with oily
   sawdust looks perfect and does nothing.
2. **The chain catcher.** A two-dollar plastic finger under the bar. It is
   the only thing between a thrown chain and your right wrist, and it snaps
   off without anyone noticing.
3. **The throttle interlock.** The palm lockout. If it is taped down — and
   people do tape it down — a dropped saw revs itself.
4. **The E-clip and washer on the clutch drum.** Behind the cover, the size
   of a fingernail. Lose it and the sprocket walks off the crank in the middle
   of a cut.

</Block>

<Block kicker="Rim versus spur sprocket">

Two kinds, and they wear differently.

- A **spur sprocket** is machined into the clutch drum. When it wears, you
  replace the drum.
- A **rim sprocket** is a separate ring that floats on the drum splines. It is
  cheap, it self-centers on the chain, and you replace just the ring.

Either way, what you look at is the depth of the wear marks. If the drive
link tangs have cut visible troughs into the sprocket teeth, it is finished —
and it will destroy a brand new chain in an afternoon while you wonder why
the new chain feels rough.

</Block>

<FailureModes kicker="The ones people get wrong">
  <Fail tell="A new chain that runs rough, jumps, or throws within the first hour. The chain isn't the problem.">
    Fitting a new chain to a worn sprocket or a worn bar.
  </Fail>
  <Fail tell="Fine dust instead of chips, a saw that needs pushing, and a bar that is hot enough to smell. Check the oiler before you blame the chain.">
    Blaming a dull chain for what is actually an oiler failure.
  </Fail>
  <Fail tell="You clean the outside of the saw and never pull the clutch cover. Everything that matters — brake band, sprocket, catcher, bar groove — is under that cover.">
    Cleaning the saw without taking the clutch cover off.
  </Fail>
</FailureModes>

<FieldLine>
Test the brake. Find the catcher. Look at the sprocket. Then start work.
</FieldLine>
