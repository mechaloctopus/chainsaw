A kickback happens in about a tenth of a second. That is faster than your
reaction time, faster than the brake, faster than any decision you might
make. Nothing about surviving one is reactive — it is all in how you were
already standing and already holding the saw.

<KickbackZones />

<Block kicker="The three reactions">

**Rotational kickback.** The chain in the **upper quadrant of the bar nose**
catches. Because the chain at that point is traveling up and back, and
because the bar is a lever pivoting on your hands, the whole saw rotates up
and back along the plane of the bar — straight toward your face and left
shoulder.

**Pull-in.** The chain on the **underside** of the bar catches — it is moving
away from you there, so it drags the saw forward into the wood. You feel it as
the saw lurching out of your grip.

**Push-back.** The chain on the **top** of the bar binds in the cut — moving
toward you, so it pushes the saw back at you. Common when the kerf closes on
the bar during a back cut or an overbuck.

</Block>

<Block kicker="What causes it">

- **Nose contact.** The upper nose touching anything: a hidden limb in the
  brush, a second stem behind the one you are cutting, the ground, a rock, a
  piece of fence.
- **Pinching.** The cut closing on the bar because you read the bind wrong.
- **Cutting above shoulder height.** Now the bar is in the plane of your head
  and you have no leverage.
- **Boring or tip-cutting without control.** The nose is where the danger
  lives, so any technique that starts with the nose demands practice and full
  attention.
- **A limb left in the kerf.** You cut through and the stub swings into the
  nose.
- **A dull chain or rakers filed too low.** Dull makes you push; low rakers
  make the chain grab.

<Hazard label="The one in storm debris">
In a tangled crown you frequently cannot see the nose of your own bar. That
is the specific condition in which kickback happens to competent people — not
carelessness, just a bar nose in a place they could not look.

If you cannot see where the nose will come out, clear the brush until you
can, or cut from the other side.
</Hazard>

</Block>

<Block kicker="How you stand, and how you hold it">

All of this is set before the chain touches wood.

<Checklist id="grip" ordered={false}>
  <Check id="thumb" why="An open grip is not a grip. The bar rotating out of an open hand is the injury mechanism in most kickback injuries to the face and shoulder.">
    **Left thumb wrapped underneath the front handle.** Always. Every cut.
  </Check>
  <Check id="elbow" why="A straight left elbow makes your arm a strut. It absorbs the rotation instead of folding and letting the saw come back.">
    **Left elbow locked straight.**
  </Check>
  <Check id="plane" why="Stand slightly to the left of the saw so your head and body are not in the arc the bar sweeps. You cannot stop a kickback — you can be somewhere else.">
    **Body out of the plane of the bar.**
  </Check>
  <Check id="hands" why="Two hands, every time, including for 'just one little cut'. Top-handle saws are designed for one-handed use by roped arborists and by nobody else.">
    **Both hands on the saw.**
  </Check>
  <Check id="feet" why="Feet apart, weight balanced, knees soft, and not standing on the log or on a round that can roll.">
    **Feet set on stable ground.**
  </Check>
  <Check id="shoulder" why="If it is above your shoulder, get a pole saw, get a lift, or leave it. There is no grip that makes overhead saw work safe.">
    **Nothing above shoulder height.**
  </Check>
  <Check id="dogs" why="The bumper spikes take the reaction of the cut so your arms don't have to, and they let you pivot the saw through instead of pushing.">
    **Dogs set into the wood.**
  </Check>
</Checklist>

</Block>

<Block kicker="The chain brake is the last line">

The brake is genuinely good and it has saved a lot of hands. It is still the
last line, not the first, and it fails in one specific, common way: a band
packed with oily sawdust, or soaked in oil, cannot clamp the drum.

So:

- **Test it running, every day.** Chain must stop.
- **Clean under the clutch cover, every day.**
- **Set the brake whenever you take a step**, whenever you hand the saw over,
  and whenever you let go of it.
- Understand that inertia activation only works if the saw is moving fast
  enough, which means a slow kickback in a tight space may never trip it.

</Block>

<FailureModes>
  <Fail tell="A thumb sitting on top of the handle. Watch your own hands for one tank and you will catch yourself doing it.">
    Losing the thumb wrap when you get tired or reach.
  </Fail>
  <Fail tell="You cannot see the nose because a leafy branch is in the way, and you cut anyway because it is only a small limb.">
    Cutting blind into brush.
  </Fail>
  <Fail tell="You are reaching up, the saw is at eye level, and you are telling yourself this is the last one.">
    Reaching overhead for a limb you could pull down from the ground.
  </Fail>
  <Fail tell="Saw bogs, you lean into it, and the nose skips. Dull is a hazard, not a nuisance.">
    Pushing a dull chain.
  </Fail>
  <Fail tell="You walk two steps with the saw idling and the chain turning. A stumble on storm ground with a live chain is the whole story.">
    Walking with the brake off.
  </Fail>
</FailureModes>
