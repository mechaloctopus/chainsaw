Two tanks, two liquids, and the two most common ways a good saw gets ruined
by a well-meaning owner: the wrong fuel, and never checking the oil.

<Block kicker="Mix">

Modern STIHL and ECHO two-stroke saws run **50:1** — one part two-stroke oil
to fifty parts gasoline — with an oil meeting the manufacturer's
specification.

<Note label="Verify it for your saw">
<Spec unverified>50:1</Spec> covers most current gas saws from both brands,
but older saws, some other brands and some oils differ, and getting it wrong
in the lean direction destroys an engine. Your model's manual is the
authority. Look it up once, write it on the fuel can with a marker, and stop
guessing.
</Note>

Use a measured mixing bottle or pre-measured oil sachets. "About a glug"
produces either a smoking, carboned-up saw or a seized one, and you will not
know which until it is too late.

</Block>

<Block kicker="Ethanol, and why it matters more here">

Pump gasoline with ethanol in it does two things small engines hate.

1. **It attracts water.** Ethanol pulls moisture out of the air. In Hawai‘i's
   humidity, a part-full can does this fast. Eventually the water and ethanol
   separate out and sit at the bottom of the tank — which is exactly where the
   fuel pickup is.
2. **It attacks rubber and plastic.** Older fuel lines, primer bulbs, carb
   diaphragms and tank vents go hard, then porous, then they leak.

Which is why:

- **Ethanol-free gasoline** is worth going out of your way for, if you can
  find it on the island.
- **Canned pre-mixed fuel** is worth the money for occasional users. It is
  expensive per gallon and cheap compared with a carburetor rebuild, and it
  keeps for years unopened.
- **Fuel stabilizer** helps if you are mixing your own and not using it fast.
- **Mix small batches.** Mixed fuel is good for a matter of weeks, not months.
  Mix what you will burn.

<Hazard label="End of the day, end of the season">
If a saw is going in the shed for more than a couple of weeks: run the tank
dry, or drain it and run the carburetor dry. A saw stored with old ethanol
fuel in it will need a carb rebuild before it runs again, and after a storm
that is a three-week wait at the dealer.
</Hazard>

</Block>

<Block kicker="Bar oil, and the test you should do every tank">

Bar oil is not motor oil. It is sticky on purpose — it has a tackifier so it
clings to a chain moving at sixty feet per second instead of flinging off.

**Do not run used motor oil.** It has no tack, it is full of the metal and
grit it collected in the engine, and you are then pumping that grit into the
bar groove.

In this climate, a heavier-weight bar oil clings better in the heat. Vegetable
based bar oils exist and work; they go rancid faster, so buy what you will
use.

### The oil-throwing test

Thirty seconds, and it is the single most useful habit in this module.

1. Start the saw and let it warm for a moment.
2. Hold the bar tip about a hand's width above a stump, a piece of cardboard
   or a light-colored piece of wood.
3. Bring it to about half throttle for two or three seconds.
4. Look for a **line of oil** thrown off the nose of the bar.

No line means no oil. Stop, and find out why before you cut: blocked oil
hole in the bar, packed groove, empty tank, blocked tank vent, or a failed
pump.

</Block>

<FailureModes>
  <Fail tell="Saw runs, cuts, then starts to smoke and push. The bar rails turn blue-gray and the chain goes slack from heat expansion. By the time you smell it the bar is done.">
    Cutting with a dead oiler.
  </Fail>
  <Fail tell="Oil tank full, fuel tank empty. Those two should empty at roughly the same rate. If they don't, the oiler is the problem — not the chain.">
    Ignoring the ratio between the two tanks.
  </Fail>
  <Fail tell="Saw starts, runs for thirty seconds, dies, and will not restart. Or it starts on choke only. Both mean fuel — old mix, water, or a blocked tank vent.">
    Fighting a fuel problem as if it were an ignition problem.
  </Fail>
  <Fail tell="A saw that smokes heavily, fouls plugs, and carbons the muffler screen. That is too much oil in the mix, and it is the mistake people make trying to be kind to the engine.">
    Guessing the mix rich.
  </Fail>
  <Fail tell="Fuel cap on the oil tank, oil cap on the fuel tank — they interchange on some saws and the vents are different. Check the caps are the right way round when you refill in a hurry.">
    Swapping the caps.
  </Fail>
</FailureModes>
