Fifteen minutes at the end of the day. It is the least interesting module on
this site and it is the one that decides whether your saw is reliable.

<Block kicker="The order">

<Checklist id="end-of-day">
  <Check n="1" why="This is the one that matters. Oily sawdust packs solid around the brake band, the sprocket and the drive pad, and a packed brake band cannot clamp. You will not see it from outside the saw.">
    **Clutch cover off.** Clean the sprocket area and the brake band.
  </Check>
  <Check n="2" why="Scrape it out end to end, then poke the oil holes clear. A blocked groove starves the bar and holds grit against the drive links.">
    **Bar groove and oil holes.**
  </Check>
  <Check n="3" why="Tap it out, brush it gently, wash it only if your filter type allows it — and then let it dry completely. A damp filter refitted tonight is a lean engine tomorrow.">
    **Air filter.** Clean, and **fully dry** before it goes back.
  </Check>
  <Check n="4" why="Packed fins mean the cylinder cannot shed heat. On a hot day with a plugged cooling system a saw will seize with everything else in perfect order.">
    **Cooling fins and flywheel area.**
  </Check>
  <Check n="5" why="A carboned screen makes the saw feel gutless and tempts people to start adjusting the carburetor.">
    **Muffler screen.**
  </Check>
  <Check n="6" why="Debris in the starter housing chews the cord and jams the pawls.">
    **Starter housing.**
  </Check>
  <Check n="7" why="Wipe the whole saw down. You are looking for cracks, loose bolts and leaks while you do it — cleaning is an inspection.">
    **Exterior, and look while you wipe.**
  </Check>
  <Check n="8" why="Both caps, and both vents. A blocked tank vent stops fuel or oil flowing and presents as a saw that dies after a minute.">
    **Caps and vents.**
  </Check>
</Checklist>

</Block>

<Block kicker="Storage in this climate">

Salt air and humidity are the two things your saw's manual was not written
for.

- **Fuel.** Going in the shed for more than two or three weeks: drain the
  tank and run the carburetor dry, or fill it with stabilized or canned
  pre-mix. Leaving pump gas with ethanol in a carburetor through a Hawai‘i
  summer is how you end up in the dealer's queue.
- **Chain tension off.** A chain left tight on a cooling bar stresses
  everything. Back it off for storage.
- **Oil the chain.** Wipe the chain with bar oil before it goes away, or take
  it off and put it in a jar of oil. Surface rust on a cutter dulls it before
  you ever cut with it again.
- **Off the floor and off concrete.** Concrete wicks moisture. A shelf, a
  pallet, or a case.
- **After a coastal day, wipe it down.** Salt spray sits on magnesium and
  aluminum and starts working immediately.

</Block>

<FailureModes>
  <Fail tell="Brake lever moves normally, brake does not stop the chain. Nobody cleaned under the cover for a month.">
    Cleaning the outside of the saw for a week and never the inside.
  </Fail>
  <Fail tell="Saw runs rough and hot after a wash. The filter went back damp and the engine is running lean on water vapor and dirt that got past it.">
    Refitting a wet air filter.
  </Fail>
  <Fail tell="Saw starts, runs thirty seconds, dies. Loosen the cap and it runs again. That is a blocked tank vent.">
    Overlooking the tank vents.
  </Fail>
  <Fail tell="Three weeks in the shed and now it will not start at all. Ethanol and humidity did that, and it is a carb job.">
    Storing a saw with pump fuel in it.
  </Fail>
</FailureModes>

<FieldLine>
Cover off, brake band clean, groove scraped, filter dry. Every day.
</FieldLine>
